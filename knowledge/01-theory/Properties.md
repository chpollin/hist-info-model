The identifiers E1–E5, M1–M4 and S1–S3 preserve the repository's proposed epistemic, medial and semiotic dimensions of historical information. Their grouping is pragmatic and incomplete. Their relationships with [[Primitives]] remain candidate associations, following [[Historical-Information#Open relationship between factors and proposals]].

## Epistemic dimensions

### E1 Uncertainty

The dissertation discusses vagueness, inconsistency, incompleteness, polyvalence and negation (Pollin, 2025, pp. 38–43). They require distinct representation and operations. A definite negative assertion, a missing value, multiple simultaneous meanings and a range of possible values are not interchangeable uncertainty degrees.

The recorded connection to Thaller's treatment of fuzziness as a system property is developed in [[Thaller-Synthesis]]. The causal relationship of these phenomena to P1–P3 remains open.

### E2 Source connection

Claims need a recoverable relationship to their evidential basis. The source-veto principle additionally concerns how evidence constrains interpretation. Provenance alone does not decide a claim's validity.

The dissertation supplies concrete structures through Bookkeeping's Entry and mention classes, Factoid/STAR's source connections and PROV-O provenance (pp. 71–79, 100–108). [[Dissertation-Context]] maintains that evidence.

### E3 Multiperspectivity

Different plausible interpretations may coexist because researchers use different methods, assumptions and source selections. The dissertation's Baltic timber example concerns such methodological differences (pp. 37–38). Its later STAR example uses illustrative unit conversions to show a representation mechanism (pp. 75–77). The two examples have different evidential roles.

### E4 Context dependence

Interpretation depends on the conditions of source creation and subsequent research. The dissertation's “bushell corn” example connects regional agricultural practices, trading standards and bookkeeping conventions (pp. 36–37). A representation must preserve the contextual distinctions relevant to the research operation.

### E5 Category contingency

Categories can change across periods and communities. The inherited example of a fifteenth-century “Kaufmann” and a contemporary “merchant” identifies a question for historical interpretation. It does not establish that the terms differ in every relevant respect. The dissertation discusses contextual political classification through changing meanings of Prussia (p. 37).

As a project proposal, category provenance should distinguish source categories from analytical categories where that distinction matters. The relationship to P1 remains unproven.

## Medial dimensions

### M1 Mediation

The medium of a source shapes what can be transmitted and examined. The existing connection to Thaller's criticism of weak typing concerns the loss of source-specific characteristics and is developed in [[Thaller-Synthesis]]. Its applicability requires comparison with concrete representations.

### M2 Intentionality

Sources were produced for particular activities and audiences. Accounting records serve practical recording and accountability purposes that differ from later historical inquiry. The relevance of those purposes to a specific omission or formulation requires evidence.

The dissertation's scholar-centred method connects source structures to research requirements (pp. 92–98). [[Absence-Modeling]] develops the repository's proposal concerning systematic omissions.

### M3 Selectivity

Sources preserve only part of past activity, and survival adds another selection process. The dissertation discusses fragmentary sources and incomplete records, including omitted payment terms and transaction details (pp. 34, 41, 55).

The proposal distinguishes incomplete attributes, selective recording and transmission loss. Their information value depends on what is known about creation and preservation.

### M4 Materiality

Physical carriers may preserve informative features that a transcription or abstract entity model loses. The carrier–content distinction therefore remains a relevant requirement candidate.

Historical measurements provide a related example of material and social practices, discussed in the dissertation on pp. 106–108. That measurement account does not by itself demonstrate the information value of a document's physical carrier. These related issues require separate evidence.

## Semiotic dimensions

### S1 Temporal complexity

Historical representations may distinguish the time of an event, the period for which an assertion holds and the time of recording or interpretation. The dissertation discusses Factoid dateForAssertion, occurredOn and hasDateAssertionMade on pp. 73–74. These model different temporal roles.

Historical calendars and reference systems are further requirement candidates. Their preservation matters when operations depend on documentary dating practices.

### S2 Semiotic layers

Abbreviations, notation, layout and writing conventions mediate source meaning. The dissertation discusses encoded bookkeeping signs and their contextual interpretation on pp. 32–36. Thaller's token concept provides an existing research connection concerning semantically rich historical datatypes.

Diplomatic reading and normalisation should remain distinguishable where the transformation affects interpretation. Whether an existing model supports that distinction requires the evidence-based comparison in [[Evaluation-Framework]].

### S3 Intertextuality

Sources may refer to other sources, share dependencies or document related transactions. The dissertation's discussion of provenance and source representation offers existing connections (pp. 77–79, 102, 105).

The recorded relationship to Thaller's factlet arrangements concerns recombination while preserving transmission context. This motivates examining source relationships, but it does not establish a necessary deduction from temporal irreversibility and trace mediation.

## Dimensions outside this grouping

The inherited grouping leaves pragmatic issues such as access restrictions, archival arrangement and digital availability underdeveloped. Social issues include scholarly schools, canon formation, power relations and gender bias in recording and interpretation.

These exclusions limit the grouping's scope. [[Absence-Modeling]] connects systematic omissions with the interpretation of missing information. No additional project scope follows automatically from listing these limits.
